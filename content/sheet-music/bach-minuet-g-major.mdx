# Minuet in G Major, BWV Anh. 114

Found in the 1725 *Notebook for Anna Magdalena Bach*, this beloved minuet was historically attributed to **Johann Sebastian Bach** for centuries. In the 1970s, musicologists discovered the piece was originally penned by Dresden court organist **Christian Petzold** as part of a harpsichord suite. 

Regardless of authorship, it remains the quintessential gateway into Baroque polyphony for pianists worldwide.

## Structural Anatomy & Form

The minuet is cast in classic **binary form (AABB)**, consisting of two 16-measure sections that each repeat.

### Section A (mm. 1–16)
* **Measures 1–4**: The opening theme announces the G Major tonality. The right hand opens with an upbeat quarter note leap (`D` up to `G`), stepping gracefully up through `A - B - C` before floating down.
* **Measures 5–8**: The theme repeats with a rhythmic variation, ending with an authentic cadence in G Major.
* **Measures 9–12**: The texture moves upward with inverted thirds in the right hand.
* **Measures 13–16**: A firm closing sequence modulating to the dominant key of **D Major** (signaled by the introduction of `C#`).

### Section B (mm. 17–32)
* The B section opens in the higher register with a descending scalar motif.
* Notice how the left hand takes on more melodic responsibility here, stepping downward in smooth quarter notes.
* The piece returns home to **G Major** with a celebratory, buoyant concluding cadence in measure 32.

## 4 Keys to an Expressive Performance

### 1. Feel the Minuet Pulse (Heavy - Light - Light)
A minuet is a French court dance. Beat 1 always carries the natural architectural weight, while beats 2 and 3 should feel buoyant and unaccented. Resist the urge to thump beats 2 and 3!

### 2. Differentiate Your Articulation
On a modern piano, we must avoid playing both hands with thick, sustained legato:
* **Right Hand**: Play the singing melody with a warm, connected **legato**.
* **Left Hand**: Play the bass quarter notes with a crisp, detached **portato** (quasi-staccato) touch, lifting between leaps like an acoustic cello bowing the bassline.

### 3. Conquering the Left-Hand Leaps
In measures 1–4, the left hand leaps between low `G`, middle `B`, and `D`.
* Never look back and forth frantically between hands.
* Practice the left hand with your eyes closed! Train the distance between finger 5 on low `G` and fingers 3 & 1 on `B` and `D` purely through muscle memory.

### 4. Ornaments (Mordents)
In measure 8 and 24, traditional Baroque performance practice often includes an upper or lower mordent on the penultimate note. 
* If you are a beginner, learn the piece **without the ornaments first**.
* Once the tempo is steady and both hands are synchronized, add the quick two-note flick right on the beat.
