Bad Bunny's *"Moscow Mule"* — the sultry, synth-drenched opening track of *Un Verano Sin Ti* — proved that reggaeton and indie-pop sensibilities can blend into an infectious keyboard groove. While the original production relies heavily on atmospheric synthesizers, clean sub-bass pulses, and vocal reverberation, the harmonic skeleton translates beautifully onto the acoustic or digital piano.

Whether you are an absolute beginner or an intermediate pianist looking to add contemporary Latin pop to your repertoire, this arrangement breaks the song down into manageable, modular layers: the harmonic progression, the iconic vocal hook melody, and the syncopated left-hand bass pattern.

## Key and chords

At its core, *"Moscow Mule"* is built on a cyclical harmonic loop that repeats throughout the verse, pre-chorus, and chorus. In standard concert pitch, the track is set in the key of **F# Minor (or A Major)**, though for beginner accessibility and ease of reading, many popular tutorials and transcriptions transpose it down one half-step into the welcoming key of **F Minor** or **E Minor**. 

For this arrangement, we will focus on the authentic **F# Minor** progression, accompanied by simple root-position shapes:

1. **F#m (F# Minor)**: `F# - A - C#`
2. **D (D Major)**: `D - F# - A`
3. **A (A Major)**: `A - C# - E`
4. **E (E Major)**: `E - G# - B`

<Callout>
**Beginner Transposition Hack**: If playing with three black keys (`F#`, `C#`, `G#`) feels daunting for your first week on the piano, place a virtual capo or transpose your digital keyboard down 2 semitones to play in **E Minor**: `Em (E - G - B) → C (C - E - G) → G (G - B - D) → D (D - F# - A)`. This gives you the identical harmonic feel using almost entirely white keys!
</Callout>

Each chord sustains for **two full measures (8 beats)** in a relaxed 4/4 tempo (approx. 100 BPM), giving your hands plenty of time to anticipate the next chord transition without rushing.

## Right-hand melody

The vocal melody delivered by Benito is rhythmic, conversational, and relies heavily on repeating syncopations around the middle register of the keyboard (Octave 4). 

The primary melodic phrase begins right on beat 2 of the measure, gently floating above the held chord:

* **Intro & Hook Motif**:
  * Over `F#m`: `C#5 - C#5 - B4 - A4 - F#4`
  * Over `D`: `F#4 - A4 - B4 - A4`
  * Over `A`: `E5 - E5 - C#5 - B4 - A4`
  * Over `E`: `B4 - B4 - A4 - G#4`

When learning the right-hand melody, keep your wrist relaxed and slightly elevated. Because Bad Bunny's cadence mimics natural speech rhythms, resist the urge to play notes with robotic, stiff staccato. Use gentle finger weight into the keybed, allowing the upper notes of the piano to sing smoothly.

## Left-hand pattern

The pulse of Latin trap and reggaeton comes from the tension between straight downbeats and syncopated off-beats (the dembow feel). On the piano, you have two distinct ways to accompany the melody with your left hand:

### 1. The Sustained Pad Pattern (Absolute Beginner)
For your first playthrough, simply strike the root note (or root-fifth power chord) in Octave 2 or 3 and hold it for the duration of each chord block:
* Measure 1–2: Single `F#2` held for 8 beats
* Measure 3–4: Single `D2` held for 8 beats
* Measure 5–6: Single `A2` held for 8 beats
* Measure 7–8: Single `E2` held for 8 beats

### 2. The Syncopated Dembow Bass (Intermediate Groove)
Once comfortable, introduce the rhythmic pulse that mimics the kick and snare groove:
* Hit the root on **Beat 1**
* Rest on beat 2
* Hit the fifth on the **"and" of beat 2**
* Hit the octave on **Beat 4**

This rhythmic displacement creates the gentle swing that makes *"Moscow Mule"* instantly recognizable on the dance floor or in the practice lounge.

## Practice tips

To master this arrangement without frustration, follow these four structured steps:

1. **Practice Hands Separately First**: Do not attempt both hands together until the right hand can hum and play the melody without hesitation.
2. **Anchor Your Thumbs**: In the right hand, keep your thumb positioned near `F#4` or `A4` as an anchor point. This prevents your hand from drifting out of position during rapid phrasing.
3. **Use a Metronome at 75 BPM**: The final song tempo is approximately 100 BPM. Start at 75% speed to lock in the rhythmic space between notes.
4. **Light Damper Pedal Technique**: Apply the sustain pedal on the downbeat of each chord change and clear it cleanly on the next downbeat to avoid harmonic mud.
