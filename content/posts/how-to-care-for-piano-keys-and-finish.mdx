A piano is more than a musical instrument — it's a piece of furniture, a significant investment, and often a treasured family heirloom. Whether you own a modest upright or a concert grand, proper care and maintenance will keep your piano looking beautiful, sounding its best, and holding its value for decades. Yet many piano owners are uncertain about how to clean and maintain their instruments, fearing they might cause damage through improper cleaning.
This guide covers everything you need to know about caring for your piano's keys and finish, from daily dusting to annual professional servicing.
## Why Piano Care Matters
### How Neglect Affects Appearance and Value
A piano that isn't properly cared for will show signs of neglect relatively quickly. Dust accumulates on the soundboard and between the strings, dulling the instrument's appearance and potentially affecting its sound. Keys become discolored from oils on players' fingers, and the wood finish can crack or fade if exposed to improper conditions. Over years of neglect, these issues compound, and what was once a beautiful instrument becomes tired and worn.
For those who view their piano as an investment, the stakes are even higher. A well-maintained piano retains its value far better than a neglected one. When it comes time to sell or trade in, the condition of the keys, finish, and interior can significantly affect the price.
### The Relationship Between Care and [Sound Quality](/blog/acoustic-vs-[digital](/blog/best-piano-metronomes-and-practice-tools)-piano-comparison)
While cleaning the exterior of a piano is primarily about appearance, interior care directly affects sound quality. Dust on the soundboard can dampen resonance slightly, and debris between the strings can cause unwanted buzzing or rattling. More importantly, humidity fluctuations that aren't managed through proper climate control can cause the soundboard to crack, the pin block to swell and shrink, and the tuning to become unstable.
### Preventive vs Reactive Maintenance
The most effective approach to piano care is preventive — addressing potential issues before they become problems. Regular dusting, climate control, and annual professional servicing prevent the vast majority of piano issues. Reactive maintenance, on the other hand, addresses problems after they occur, which is always more expensive and less effective.
<Callout>
**An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure.** A [humidity control](/blog/piano-maintenance-and-tuning-guide) system that costs $200 can prevent a soundboard crack that would cost thousands to repair. Invest in prevention, and your piano will reward you with decades of beautiful music.
</Callout>
## Cleaning Piano Keys
### Cleaning Ivory Keys — Gentle Methods
If your piano has genuine ivory keys (common on pianos built before the 1950s), they require special care. Ivory is a natural material that is porous and can be damaged by harsh chemicals or excessive moisture. To clean ivory keys:
1. Use a soft, lint-free cloth slightly dampened with water. Wring out all excess moisture — the cloth should be barely wet.
2. Wipe each key individually from back to front (away from the space between keys to avoid pushing moisture into the key bed).
3. Dry immediately with a separate soft cloth.
4. For stubborn stains, a tiny amount of mild soap can be used, but avoid any abrasive cleaners or chemicals.
Ivory keys can also develop a yellowish tint over time. This is natural aging and cannot be reversed without professional refinishing. Avoid attempting to whiten ivory keys with bleach or other chemicals, as this will damage the ivory.
### Cleaning Plastic and Synthetic Keys
Most modern pianos have plastic or synthetic ivory key surfaces. These are more durable than natural ivory and can be cleaned more aggressively:
1. Use a soft cloth dampened with a mild soap solution or a dedicated piano key cleaner.
2. Wipe each key from back to front, drying immediately.
3. For disinfecting, use a cloth lightly dampened with isopropyl alcohol (70% or less). Don't pour alcohol directly on the keys — apply it to the cloth first.
<Note>
Clean one key at a time, or at most a few keys at once. The goal is to prevent any moisture from seeping between the keys into the key bed, where it can cause swelling or warping.
</Note>
### What NOT to Use — Harmful Chemicals to Avoid
Certain cleaning products will damage piano keys regardless of material:
- **Bleach and ammonia**: These harsh chemicals will discolor and degrade both ivory and plastic keys.
- **Abrasive cleaners**: Any cleaner with scrubbing particles will scratch the key surface.
- **Excessive water**: Pools of water seeping between keys will damage the wood underneath.
- **Furniture polish**: Polish leaves a residue that makes keys slippery and can transfer to the felt key bushings, causing them to stick.
- **Acetone or nail polish remover**: These will dissolve plastic key surfaces instantly.
## Maintaining the Piano Finish
### Polishing Lacquer and Polyester Finishes
Most modern pianos have either a lacquer or polyester finish. Lacquer finishes (common on American and some [European](/blog/best-piano-brands-comparison-guide) pianos) are thinner and more delicate. Polyester finishes (common on Asian pianos) are thicker, harder, and more resistant to scratches.
For lacquer finishes, use a soft cloth and a small amount of piano-specific polish. Apply in a circular motion, then buff with a clean cloth. Avoid polishing too frequently — once or twice a year is sufficient. Over-polishing can build up a residue that dulls the finish.
For polyester finishes, a microfiber cloth and a dedicated piano polish designed for high-gloss finishes will restore the shine. Polyester finishes are very durable and can also be lightly polished with a cloth dampened with water for routine cleaning.
### Dusting and Wiping Techniques
For routine dusting, use a soft, dry microfiber cloth or a lambswool duster. Dust in the direction of the wood grain (if visible) and avoid pressing hard — let the cloth do the work. For more thorough cleaning, use a cloth slightly dampened with water, then dry immediately.
Never use feather dusters on a piano finish — they can scratch the surface. Similarly, avoid paper towels, which are surprisingly abrasive and can leave micro-scratches on glossy finishes over time.
### Protecting Against Humidity and Sunlight
Two environmental factors cause the most damage to piano finishes: humidity fluctuations and direct sunlight. Humidity causes the wood to expand and contract, which can crack the finish. Sunlight bleaches the color out of wood and can cause lacquer finishes to check (develop small cracks).
Position your piano away from windows where direct sunlight falls on it, or use UV-filtering window treatments. Maintain consistent humidity levels in the room (ideally 40-50% relative humidity) using a humidifier in winter and a dehumidifier in summer.
<Callout>
**Never place drinks on your piano.** A spilled glass of water on the finish is bad enough, but liquid seeping into the interior can cause catastrophic damage. Keep the piano lid closed when not in use to protect the interior from dust, spills, and pets.
</Callout>
## Interior Care and Climate Control
### Soundboard and String Maintenance
The interior of your piano — the soundboard, strings, and [action](/blog/upright-piano-vs-grand-piano-sound-difference) — requires professional attention for most maintenance tasks. However, you can gently dust the soundboard (the large wooden panel under the strings) using a soft brush or cloth on a flexible wand. Be extremely careful not to touch the strings, as oils from your skin will cause them to corrode.
If you notice dust accumulation between the strings or on the tuning pins, a piano technician can clean these areas during a regular service visit. Don't attempt to vacuum or blow compressed air into the piano interior — this can push dust deeper into the action mechanism.
### Humidity Control Systems
The single most important factor in piano longevity is humidity control. Fluctuations in humidity cause the soundboard to swell and shrink, which affects tuning stability and can eventually lead to cracks. A dedicated piano humidity control system (such as a Dampp-Chaser Piano Life Saver) installed inside the piano maintains consistent humidity levels regardless of room conditions.
If an internal system isn't feasible, a room humidifier and dehumidifier will help. Monitor humidity with a hygrometer and aim for 40-50% relative humidity year-round.
### When to Call a Professional Technician
Some maintenance tasks should only be performed by a qualified piano technician:
- Tuning (every 6-12 months)
- Regulation (adjusting the action mechanism, every 5-10 years)
- Voicing (adjusting hammer hardness for tone, as needed)
- String replacement
- Soundboard repair
- Action repair or replacement
## Creating a Maintenance Schedule
### Daily and Weekly Care Tasks
- **Daily**: Close the keyboard cover when not in use. Keep the piano area clean and dust-free.
- **Weekly**: Dust the exterior with a soft microfiber cloth. Check that the piano area maintains consistent temperature and humidity.
### Monthly and Seasonal Maintenance
- **Monthly**: Inspect keys for any sticking or sluggishness. Check humidity levels with a hygrometer.
- **Seasonally**: Adjust humidifier/dehumidifier settings as outdoor humidity changes. Lightly clean keys if needed.
### Annual Professional Servicing [Checklist](/blog/piano-moving-and-transportation-guide)
- Tuning (at minimum once per year, ideally twice)
- Inspection of the action, strings, and soundboard
- Cleaning of the interior by the technician
- Assessment of humidity control system
- Evaluation of any needed regulation or voicing
<Note>
**Keep a maintenance log.** Record each tuning, repair, and service visit with the date and technician's name. This log is valuable for tracking your piano's health over time and provides documentation if you ever sell the instrument.
</Note>
Proper piano care is not complicated, but it requires consistency. By establishing a simple routine of dusting, climate control, and annual professional servicing, you'll ensure that your piano remains a beautiful, reliable, and valuable instrument for generations. The few minutes you spend each week on maintenance will pay dividends in decades of musical enjoyment.
