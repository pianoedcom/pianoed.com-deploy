A piano is one of the heaviest, most delicate, and most awkward objects in any home. Moving one is not a DIY afterthought — it is a job that rewards careful planning and punishes shortcuts. A dropped piano can mean thousands in repairs, a cracked [soundboard](/blog/acoustic-piano-soundboard-explained), or a total loss. Whether you're moving across the room or across the country, this guide walks you through how to do it safely.
## Why Pianos Are So Hard to Move
Pianos combine extreme weight with fragile internals. An upright can weigh 300–500 pounds; a grand can top 1,000. Inside, thousands of parts — strings under high tension, a wooden soundboard, delicate hammers, and a precisely regulated [action](/blog/upright-piano-vs-grand-piano-sound-difference) — can be damaged by a single jolt. The finish scratches easily, and the legs and pedals of a grand are vulnerable to sideways stress.
<Callout type="warning">
Never move a grand piano by its legs. The legs are designed to bear vertical weight, not lateral force. Lifting or pushing a grand by a leg can snap it off and topple the instrument.
</Callout>
Understanding this combination of weight and fragility is the first step to moving safely.
## Decide: Hire Professionals or DIY?
For most pianos, hiring specialist piano movers is the wise [choice](/blog/best-piano-for-small-apartment-spaces). They have the equipment (piano boards, straps, ramps, dollies), the experience, and insurance. The cost is modest compared to the value of the instrument and the risk of damage.
Consider DIY only for:
- Small spinet or console uprights moving within the same floor
- Short, flat moves with no stairs
- Players with proper equipment and at least four strong helpers
For anything involving stairs, a grand piano, or a vehicle transport, hire professionals.
## Preparation Checklist
Before moving day, prepare both the piano and the route:
- [ ] **Tape the keyboard lid down** — gently, with painter's tape, so it doesn't fly open
- [ ] **Lock the lid** (if the piano has a lock) or secure it closed
- [ ] **Wrap the piano** in moving blankets, [padding](/blog/best-piano-covers-and-cases) all corners and edges
- [ ] **Protect the pedals** — wrap them so they aren't bent
- [ ] **Measure doorways and hallways** — confirm the piano fits through every opening on the route
- [ ] **Clear the path** — remove rugs, furniture, and anything you could trip over
- [ ] **Check stairs and landings** — ensure they can bear the weight and have room to maneuver
- [ ] **Plan the vehicle** — use a truck with a lift gate and tie-down points
- [ ] **Check the weather** — avoid rain, snow, or extreme heat on moving day
## Moving an Upright Piano
1. **Wrap and pad** the entire piano, securing blankets with straps (not tape on the finish)
2. **Tilt and load onto a dolly** — use a four-wheel piano dolly rated for the weight
3. **Strap the piano to the dolly** so it cannot shift
4. **Move slowly** — one person guides, others watch corners and obstacles
5. **For stairs**, use a piano board (a specialized skid with straps) and at least four people
6. **Load onto the truck** via ramp or lift gate, never by lifting the whole piano
7. **Secure in the truck** with heavy straps to the truck's tie-down points, braced so it cannot tip
## Moving a Grand Piano
Grands require special handling:
1. **Remove the lid** (if possible) or secure it firmly closed
2. **Lower the lid and pad it**
3. **Remove the legs and pedal lyre** — grand pianos are moved on their side on a piano board
4. **Carefully tip the piano** onto the board with enough helpers (at least four)
5. **Strap the piano to the board** securely
6. **Pad all exposed surfaces** — especially the finish and the action
7. **Move and load** using the board, keeping the piano on its long side
8. **Reassemble at the destination** — reattach legs and pedals with the piano properly supported
This is genuinely a job for professionals. The risk of damage from incorrect tipping or reassembly is high.
## Transportation Tips
- **Keep the piano upright** during transport whenever possible — uprights travel upright; grands travel on their side on a board
- **Avoid sudden stops** — drive slowly and smoothly
- **Secure against shifting** — a piano that slides in the truck can destroy itself and the truck
- **Protect from temperature and [humidity](/blog/piano-maintenance-and-tuning-guide)** — avoid leaving the piano in a hot or cold truck for long; [humidity and climate](/blog/piano-humidity-control-and-climate) swings can crack wood and detune strings
- **Don't lay a piano on its back** for long periods — it stresses the action and pinblock
## At the Destination
1. **Let the piano settle** — after a move, a piano needs time to adjust to the new temperature and humidity before tuning
2. **Wait at least two weeks before tuning** — tuning immediately wastes money as the piano continues to shift
3. **Place it carefully** — away from exterior walls, heating vents, direct sunlight, and windows
4. **Inspect for damage** — check the finish, pedals, keys, and action; note any issues for insurance
5. **Schedule a [professional](/blog/how-to-care-for-piano-keys-and-finish) inspection** if the move was long or rough — a technician can catch hidden problems
<Callout type="tip">
After any significant move, budget for a tuning and possibly a regulation check. Even a careful move disturbs the piano's delicate balance, and a fresh tuning restores both sound and your peace of mind.
</Callout>
## Insurance and Liability
Confirm coverage before the move:
- **Homeowner's insurance** may not cover pianos in transit — check your policy
- **Movers' insurance** — verify what the moving company covers and the deductible
- **Specialty piano movers** often include insurance; clarify the terms in writing
- **Document the piano's condition** with photos before and after the move
## Common Mistakes to Avoid
- **Using too few people** — undermanned moves are how pianos get dropped
- **Skipping the blankets** — even a gentle brush against a doorframe scratches the finish
- **Moving in extreme weather** — heat, cold, and moisture all damage pianos
- **Tuning too soon** — wastes a tuning and can stress the instrument
- **Forgetting to measure** — a piano that doesn't fit through a doorway mid-move is a crisis
Moving a piano is a serious undertaking, but with preparation, the right help, and respect for the instrument's weight and fragility, it can be done safely. Treat the move as carefully as you treat the music, and your piano will arrive ready to play for years to come.
