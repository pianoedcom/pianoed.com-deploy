Choosing a piano method book might seem like a minor decision, but the method you follow shapes your entire learning journey. The right method provides a structured path from your first notes to confident playing, while the wrong one can leave you frustrated or stalled. The good news is that several excellent, time-tested methods are available, each with its own philosophy and approach.
This guide compares the most [popular](/blog/pop-piano-chord-charts-for-popular-songs) piano method books to help you find the one that matches your learning style, age, and goals.
## What Makes a Great Piano Method Book
A method book is more than a collection of pieces — it's a carefully sequenced curriculum that builds skills progressively. The best method books share several key characteristics.
**Structured progression and pacing** are essential. A good method introduces concepts in a logical order, ensuring each new skill builds on the previous one. You should never feel thrown into the deep end. The pacing should challenge you without overwhelming you, with enough repetition to solidify each concept before moving on.
A **balance of theory, technique, and [repertoire](/blog/classical-piano-repertoire-by-era-guide)** keeps learning engaging. Methods that focus solely on technique become dry, while those that only offer pieces without explaining the underlying theory leave gaps in your understanding. The best methods weave all three together, so you learn a new chord, understand why it works, and immediately play a piece that uses it.
**Accompanying audio and digital [resources](/blog/free-piano-sheet-music-download-resources)** have become increasingly important. Many modern methods include backing tracks, interactive apps, and video demonstrations that make practice more engaging and help you develop your ear by playing along with accompaniment.
## Alfred's Basic Piano Library
Alfred's Basic Piano Library is one of the most widely used piano methods in the world, particularly in North America. Written by Willard Palmer, Morton Manus, and Amanda Vick Lethco, it has been a staple of piano teaching since the 1970s.
### Structure and Approach
Alfred's method is known for its thorough, incremental approach. It introduces new concepts one at a time with clear explanations and ample practice material. The method uses a multi-key approach, teaching students to play in multiple keys relatively early, rather than staying in C major for an extended period.
The core series includes Lesson, Theory, Technic, and Recital books at each level, creating a comprehensive system when used together. The Lesson book is the primary text, while the supplementary books reinforce and expand on each concept.
### Adult vs Children Versions
Alfred offers distinct versions for different age groups. Alfred's Basic Piano Library is designed for children ages 7-12, while Alfred's Premier Piano Course is a newer, more modern alternative with updated repertoire and full-color illustrations. For adults, [Alfred's Adult All](/blog/best-piano-books-for-self-taught-adults)-in-One Course combines lesson, theory, and technique in a single volume, making it ideal for self-study.
<Callout>
**Best for:** Students who want a thorough, traditional approach with clear structure. Alfred is particularly well-suited for self-taught adults using the All-in-One Course.
</Callout>
### Strengths and Limitations
Alfred's strengths lie in its comprehensive coverage, clear explanations, and wide availability. The supplementary materials are extensive, and the method has been refined over decades of classroom use. However, some find the repertoire somewhat dated, and the method can feel rigid for students who prefer a more creative, exploratory approach.
## Faber Piano Adventures
Written by Nancy and Randall Faber, Piano Adventures has become the method of choice for many contemporary piano teachers. First published in the 1990s, it represents a more modern approach to piano pedagogy.
### The Faber Approach to Learning
Piano Adventures is built on the philosophy that students learn best when they are engaged, motivated, and making music from the very first lesson. The method emphasizes musicality alongside technique, encouraging students to play expressively from the beginning rather than treating expression as an advanced skill.
The Fabers incorporate a wide range of musical styles — [classical](/blog/classical-piano-sheet-music-for-beginners), folk, pop, and jazz — giving students a broad musical foundation. The method also integrates theory and technique directly into the lesson material, rather than treating them as separate subjects.
### Level Structure and Supplementary Books
Piano Adventures is organized into a clear progression: Primer Level, Level 1, Level 2A, Level 2B, Level 3A, Level 3B, Level 4, and Level 5. Each level includes a Lesson book, Theory book, Performance book, and Technique & Artistry book. The Fabers also offer the Adult Piano Adventures series, which condenses the method into a format suitable for older beginners.
<Note>
Piano Adventures is widely praised by teachers for its engaging repertoire and thoughtful pedagogy. The Fabers' background in both performance and psychology informs a method that understands how students actually learn.
</Note>
## Suzuki, Bastien, and Other Methods
### Suzuki Method — Ear-First Philosophy
The Suzuki method, developed by Shinichi Suzuki, is based on the principle that children learn music the same way they learn language — through immersion, listening, and imitation. Students begin by listening to recordings of the pieces they will learn, then reproduce them by ear before learning to read notation.
The Suzuki method is primarily designed for young children (ages 3-7) and requires active parental involvement. While it excels at developing ear training and musical sensitivity, it has been criticized for delaying note-reading, which can create challenges when students transition to more advanced repertoire.
### Bastien Method for Young Beginners
The Bastien method, written by James Bastien, is another classic approach particularly popular for young children. The Bastien Piano Basics series is known for its colorful illustrations, simple explanations, and gradual pacing. It is often used with children ages 5-7 who need a gentle introduction to the piano.
### Hal Leonard and Other Notable Methods
Hal Leonard Student Piano Library is a more recent method that combines elements of traditional and modern approaches. It features engaging repertoire, interactive online resources, and a well-structured progression. Other notable methods include John Thompson's Modern Course (a classic method with a traditional, no-nonsense approach) and the Royal Conservatory of Music series (a rigorous, exam-oriented curriculum popular in Canada and increasingly worldwide).
## Choosing the Right Method for You
For **self-taught adults**, Alfred's Adult All-in-One Course or Adult Piano Adventures are the most popular choices. Both are designed for independent study and include theory alongside the lesson material. Alfred tends to be more thorough and traditional, while Faber is more engaging and modern.
For **children learning with a teacher**, Piano Adventures is the most recommended method by contemporary teachers, though Alfred and Bastien remain solid choices. The best method is often the one your teacher is most experienced with.
For **students who want a creative, ear-based approach**, the Suzuki method offers a unique philosophy, particularly for young children. However, supplementing with a traditional method for note-reading is essential for long-term development.
<Callout>
**Don't be afraid to combine methods.** Many teachers use a primary method and supplement with repertoire from other sources. The goal is not to follow a single method perfectly, but to develop well-rounded musicianship.
</Callout>
Ultimately, the best method book is the one you'll actually use consistently. A method that sits on a shelf unread is worthless, regardless of its pedagogical merits. Choose a method that inspires you to sit down and practice, and you'll be well on your way to a lifetime of piano enjoyment.
