If you've never played the piano before, the path forward can feel overwhelming. Where do you start? What should you learn first? How long until you can play a song you actually enjoy? This guide gives you a clear, step-by-step roadmap — from your very first day at the keyboard to playing your first real pieces with confidence.
You don't need talent, a [teacher](/blog/how-to-choose-a-piano-teacher) (though one helps), or a lot of money. You need a piano, a little time each day, and patience. Let's walk the path together.
## Phase 1: Getting Oriented (Weeks 1–2)
### What to Learn
Before you play a single note, get to know your instrument:
- **The keyboard layout** — Find middle C. Understand that the pattern of white and black keys repeats every 12 notes (an octave)
- **Posture and hand position** — Sit at the right height, feet flat, wrists level, fingers curved as if [holding](/blog/how-to-build-piano-finger-[independence](/blog/best-piano-finger-exercises-for-beginners)) a small ball
- **Finger numbers** — Thumb is 1, index is 2, middle is 3, ring is 4, pinky is 5 (both hands)
- **The C major five-finger position** — Right hand thumb on C, left hand pinky on C
### What to Practice
- [ ] Find and name all the C's on your keyboard
- [ ] Play each finger in sequence (1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2-1) with each hand separately
- [ ] Practice playing with curved fingers (no flat fingers!)
- [ ] Sit at the piano and check your posture in a mirror
<Callout type="tip">
Don't rush this phase. Good posture and hand position are the foundation of everything that follows. Bad habits formed here are hard to unlearn later.
</Callout>
### Milestone
You can find any note on the keyboard, name the finger numbers, and sit with correct posture.
## Phase 2: First Notes and Rhythms (Weeks 3–4)
### What to Learn
- **Note names** — Learn the white keys: C-D-E-F-G-A-B, then back to C
- **Basic rhythm** — Quarter notes (1 beat), half notes (2 beats), whole notes (4 beats)
- **Reading on the staff** — Treble clef (right hand) and bass clef (left hand)
- **Simple [melodies](/blog/how-to-play-piano-by-ear)** — Songs using only C-D-E-F-G in the right hand
### What to Practice
- [ ] Play "Mary Had a Little Lamb" (E-D-C-D-E-E-E) with your right hand
- [ ] Play "Ode to Joy" using C-D-E-F-G
- [ ] Practice counting quarter notes out loud while playing
- [ ] Start a beginner method book (Alfred's, Faber, or Bastien)
### Milestone
You can play simple melodies in C position with one hand at a time, reading from the staff.
## Phase 3: Hands Together (Weeks 5–8)
### What to Learn
- **Playing with both hands** — Right hand plays melody, left hand plays simple accompaniment
- **C and G7 chords** — The two chords that unlock dozens of simple songs
- **[Chord changes](/blog/how-to-accompany-yourself-singing-on-piano)** — Switching between C and G7 smoothly in the left hand
### What to Practice
- [ ] Play a melody in the right hand while holding a C chord in the left
- [ ] Switch between C and G7 chords in the left hand at a slow tempo
- [ ] Play "When the Saints Go Marching In" with melody and chords
- [ ] Practice 10 minutes of hands-together playing daily
<Callout type="note">
Playing hands together is the biggest hurdle for beginners. It will feel impossible at first. It isn't — your brain just needs time to coordinate two hands doing different things. Be patient. It clicks.
</Callout>
### Milestone
You can play a simple song with melody in the right hand and chords in the left, hands together, at a steady tempo.
## Phase 4: Expanding the Keyboard (Weeks 9–12)
### What to Learn
- **Sharps and flats** — Black keys and how they modify notes
- **New keys** — G major (one sharp: F#) and F major (one flat: Bb)
- **Scales** — C major scale, one octave, hands separately
- **More chords** — F, Am, Dm — the chords that unlock pop songs
### What to Practice
- [ ] Play the C major scale, one octave, right hand only, then left hand only
- [ ] Learn the I-V-vi-IV chord progression (C-G-Am-F) — the most common pop progression
- [ ] Play a simple pop song using chord symbols
- [ ] Practice in G major and F major positions
### Milestone
You can play the C major scale, know basic chords in multiple keys, and can play a simple pop song from a chord chart.
## Phase 5: [Building](/blog/how-to-memorize-piano-pieces-faster) Fluency (Months 4–6)
### What to Learn
- **Two-octave scales** — C, G, F major, hands together
- **Arpeggios** — C major arpeggio, hands separately
- **Dynamics** — Playing loud (forte) and soft (piano) with control
- **Pedaling** — Using the sustain pedal for legato
- **More repertoire** — Easier pieces from classical, pop, and jazz
### What to Practice
- [ ] Daily scale practice (10 minutes) — C, G, F major, two octaves
- [ ] Learn a piece you genuinely love, even if it's slightly challenging
- [ ] Practice dynamics — play the same melody loud, then soft, then with a crescendo
- [ ] Start using the sustain pedal on simple pieces
<Callout type="tip">
At this stage, the most important thing is to play music you enjoy. If you only practice exercises, you'll quit. Find songs you love and learn them — even simplified versions. The joy of playing real music is what keeps you going.
</Callout>
### Milestone
You can play scales in multiple keys, use the pedal, control dynamics, and have a small repertoire of pieces you enjoy playing.
## Phase 6: Independence and Exploration (Months 7–12)
### What to Learn
- **All 12 major scales** — One octave, hands together
- **Minor scales** — Natural, harmonic, and melodic minor
- **Chord inversions** — Playing the same chord in different positions
- **Reading lead sheets** — Melody + chord symbols
- **Your first "real" classical piece** — A simplified sonatina or Bach minuet
### What to Practice
- [ ] Learn all 12 major scales over the next 3 months (one new key per week)
- [ ] Practice chord inversions for C, F, G, Am, Dm
- [ ] Learn one new piece per month from a genre you love
- [ ] Start improvising — make up melodies over a chord progression
### Milestone
You have a solid foundation: scales in 12 keys, chord vocabulary, reading skills, dynamics, pedaling, and a growing repertoire. You're no longer a beginner — you're an intermediate player finding your voice.
## How Long Does It Take?
Everyone asks this, and the honest answer is: it depends on how much you practice. But here's a rough guide:
| Practice Time | Time to Phase 3 | Time to Phase 6 |
|---|---|---|
| 15 min/day | 3 months | 18–24 months |
| 30 min/day | 2 months | 12–15 months |
| 60 min/day | 1 month | 8–12 months |
The key is consistency. Fifteen minutes every day is far more effective than two hours once a week.
## What You Need to Get Started
- **A piano or keyboard** — 88 weighted keys is ideal, but 61 keys is fine for the first few months. See our [Best Beginner Keyboard Piano Under $500](/blog/best-beginner-keyboard-piano-under-500) guide.
- **A method book** — Alfred's Adult All-in-One Course or Faber Adult Piano Adventures
- **A metronome** — A phone app is fine to start
- **A bench at the right height** — See our [Piano Bench Buying Guide](/blog/piano-bench-buying-guide-for-proper-posture)
- **15–30 minutes a day** — That's all it takes to start
