Every athlete warms up before training. Pianists are athletes of the small muscles — and those muscles need the same care. A good warm-up [routine](/blog/piano-warm-up-exercises-routine) prepares your fingers, wrists, arms, and mind for focused practice, reduces the risk of injury, and makes everything you play afterward feel easier.
Here's a complete warm-up checklist you can run through in 10–15 minutes before any practice session.
## Why Warm Up?
- **Prevents injury** — Cold hands are more prone to strain, tendonitis, and repetitive stress injuries
- **Improves dexterity** — Warm muscles move [faster](/blog/how-to-memorize-piano-pieces-faster) and more accurately
- **Focuses the mind** — A warm-up routine signals to your brain that it's time to concentrate
- **Sets technique baseline** — Scales and arpeggios remind your hands of proper form before you tackle repertoire
<Callout type="note">
If you've been typing, driving, or doing other hand-intensive activities, your hands may feel "warm" but not "piano-warm." Piano requires specific [finger independence](/blog/how-to-build-piano-finger-independence) and wrist flexibility that everyday activities don't engage.
</Callout>
## The 10-Minute Warm-Up Checklist
### Step 1: Physical Warm-Up (2 minutes)
Before touching the keys, wake up your hands and arms:
- [ ] **Hand stretches** — Gently spread fingers wide, hold for 5 seconds, relax. Repeat 5 times each hand
- [ ] **Wrist circles** — Rotate wrists slowly clockwise 5 times, then counterclockwise 5 times
- [ ] **Finger shakes** — Shake hands loosely as if flicking water off your fingertips for 30 seconds
- [ ] **Forearm stretch** — Extend one arm, gently pull fingers back with the other hand, hold 15 seconds. Repeat on both sides
- [ ] **Shoulder rolls** — Roll shoulders backward 5 times, forward 5 times — tension here travels to your hands
### Step 2: Five-Finger Patterns (2 minutes)
Place your right hand in C position (thumb on C, fingers 2-5 on D-E-F-G) and play slowly:
- [ ] **Five-finger exercise** — Play C-D-E-F-G-F-E-D-C slowly, 1-2-3-4-5-4-3-2-1, at 60 BPM
- [ ] **Repeat in left hand** — Same pattern, left hand in C position
- [ ] **Hands together** — Play the same pattern with both hands simultaneously
- [ ] **Move to G position** — Shift to G-A-B-C-D and repeat
- [ ] **Move to F position** — Shift to F-G-A-Bb-C and repeat
<Callout type="tip">
Focus on evenness, not speed. Every note should have the same volume and duration. This is harder than it sounds — and more valuable than it seems.
</Callout>
### Step 3: Scales (3 minutes)
Scales are the single most valuable warm-up exercise. They build finger strength, coordination, and keyboard geography.
- [ ] **C major, hands separately** — One octave, right hand, then left hand, at 70 BPM
- [ ] **C major, hands together** — One octave, both hands, at 70 BPM
- [ ] **G major** — One octave, hands together (remember F#)
- [ ] **F major** — One octave, hands together (remember Bb)
- [ ] **D major** — One octave, hands together (F# and C#)
- [ ] **A minor (natural)** — One octave, hands together
If you're comfortable with these, extend to two octaves and increase the tempo gradually.
### Step 4: Arpeggios (2 minutes)
Arpeggios train your hand to open and close across the keyboard — essential for repertoire.
- [ ] **C major arpeggio, right hand** — C-E-G-C, one octave, at 60 BPM
- [ ] **C major arpeggio, left hand** — Same pattern, left hand
- [ ] **C major arpeggio, hands together** — Both hands, one octave
- [ ] **A minor arpeggio** — A-C-E-A, hands together
- [ ] **G major arpeggio** — G-B-D-G, hands together
### Step 5: Hanon or Czerny (1 minute)
Pick one exercise from Hanon or Czerny to focus on finger independence:
- [ ] **Hanon No. 1** — The classic five-finger exercise, played at a moderate tempo
- [ ] **Czerny Op. 599 No. 1** — Simple but excellent for evenness
<Callout type="tip">
Don't race through Hanon. Play it slowly enough that every finger has equal weight and every note is perfectly even. Speed comes from accuracy, not the other way around.
</Callout>
### Step 6: Mental Preparation (1 minute)
Before diving into repertoire, take 60 seconds to set your intention:
- [ ] **Review your practice goals** — What are you working on today?
- [ ] **Visualize the first piece** — Hear it in your mind before playing
- [ ] **Set a tempo** — Decide the tempo for your first piece before you start
- [ ] **Check your posture** — Sit up, feet flat, wrists level, shoulders relaxed
## Customizing Your Warm-Up
### For Beginners (5-minute version)
If 10 minutes feels like too much, start with:
1. Hand stretches (1 min)
2. Five-finger patterns (2 min)
3. One scale, hands together (1 min)
4. Mental check (1 min)
### For Intermediate Players (15-minute version)
Add:
- All 12 major scales, two octaves
- Minor scales (harmonic and melodic)
- Arpeggios in all keys
- Hanon exercises 1–5
### For Advanced Players (20+ minutes)
Add:
- Four-octave scales in all keys, parallel and contrary motion
- Arpeggios in all inversions
- Chromatic scales, hands together
- Czerny or Cramer studies
## Common Warm-Up Mistakes
- **Playing too fast** — Warm-ups are about control, not speed. Slow down.
- **Skipping the physical warm-up** — Your hands need blood flow before you start playing.
- **Playing on autopilot** — Pay attention to every note. Mindless repetition reinforces bad habits.
- **Skipping scales** — Scales are the foundation. Even professionals play them daily.
- **Warming up too long** — If your warm-up takes 30 minutes, you're practicing technique, not warming up. Keep it focused.
