# Hanon Exercise No. 1

Charles-Louis Hanon's *The Virtuoso Pianist in 60 Exercises* was composed to prepare students for the full range of keyboard literature. **Exercise No. 1** is designed to stretch and liberate fingers 4 and 5 (the ring and little fingers), which naturally share tendon connections in the hand.

## Overview & Pattern Logic

The beauty of Hanon No. 1 lies in its mathematical simplicity. Once you master the contour in the first measure, your fingers follow the identical interval shape all the way up the keyboard and back down.

### The Ascending Pattern
1. **Right Hand**: Skip a note from **C4** up to **E4** (fingers 1 to 2), then step up sequentially: **F4** (3), **G4** (4), **A4** (5). Turn around and step back down: **G4** (4), **F4** (3), **E4** (2).
2. **Left Hand**: Skip a note from **C3** up to **E3** (fingers 5 to 4), then step up: **F3** (3), **G3** (2), **A3** (1), stepping down: **G3** (2), **F3** (3), **E3** (4).
3. Shift up by one white key (**D**) and repeat the identical finger sequence. Continue ascending until you reach high **B**.

### The Descending Pattern
At the peak (high B), the pattern flips:
1. Skip down a step from **G5** to **E5** (fingers 5 to 4 in RH, 1 to 2 in LH).
2. Step downward sequentially, then return back up.
3. Continue descending step by step until you return to the starting **C**.

## Common Pitfalls & How to Avoid Them

### 1. The "Ghost Note" on Finger 4
Because the 4th finger shares the *extensor digitorum* tendon with finger 3, beginners often drop it too weakly or delay its strike. 
* **Fix**: Practice with exaggerated high finger lifting at a very slow tempo (40–50 BPM), making sure finger 4 strikes with the same firm fingertip contact as finger 2.

### 2. Collapsing Wrist on Finger 5
When reaching the top note with the 5th finger, the outer wrist frequently sags downward toward the keybed.
* **Fix**: Imagine your forearm is a straight bridge supporting your hand. Rotate the forearm slightly into finger 5 rather than collapsing the wrist.
