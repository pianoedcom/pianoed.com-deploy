# Practice Exercise Database
A curated collection of piano exercises with practice tips, tempo guidelines, and common mistakes to avoid. Each exercise includes annotations to help you get the most out of your practice time.
## Hanon Exercises
### Hanon No. 1 — Five-Finger Pattern
**Pattern:** C-D-E-F-G-F-E-D-C, then shift up one note and repeat.
**Practice Tips:**
- Start at quarter note = 60 BPM
- Focus on absolute evenness — every note the same volume
- Keep fingers close to the keys
- Transpose to all 12 major keys (not just C major!)
<Callout type="warning">
The most common Hanon mistake is playing too fast. Speed comes from control, not effort. If you can't play perfectly at a slow tempo, you can't play perfectly at a fast one.
</Callout>
### Hanon No. 2 — Ascending/Descending
**Pattern:** C-D-E-F-G-F-E-D-C then E-D-C-B-A-B-C-D-E
**Practice Tips:**
- Emphasize the contrast between ascending (crescendo) and descending (diminuendo)
- Watch the thumb crossing — keep it light
- Practice with staccato articulation for finger independence
## Czerny Exercises
### Czerny Op. 599, No. 1
**Focus:** Right hand melodic patterns over left hand chords.
**Practice Tips:**
- Practice hands separately first
- Pay attention to phrasing — Czerny is musical, not just technical
- Use as a sight-reading exercise after mastering
### Czerny Op. 599, No. 6
**Focus:** Five-finger position with simple accompaniment.
**Practice Tips:**
- Emphasize the melodic line in the right hand
- Keep the left hand chords soft and supportive
- Practice with a metronome for rhythmic stability
## Scale Exercises
### Major Scales — All 12 Keys
**Pattern:** One octave, hands together, contrary motion, then parallel motion.
**Practice Tips:**
- Start with C, G, D, A, E (sharp keys), then F, Bb, Eb (flat keys)
- Practice at quarter note = 80 BPM, one note per beat
- Focus on even thumb crossings
- Gradually increase to two, three, four octaves
### Harmonic Minor Scales
**Pattern:** Same as major but with raised 7th degree.
**Practice Tips:**
- The augmented 2nd interval (between 6th and 7th) is the challenge
- Practice slowly to develop the stretch
- Listen for the distinctive harmonic minor sound
## Arpeggio Exercises
### Root Position Major Arpeggios
**Pattern:** Root-3rd-5th-octave, hands together.
**Practice Tips:**
- Practice the thumb crossing separately
- Keep the wrist flexible — don't lock it
- Aim for a flowing, harp-like sound
## Daily Exercise Routine
<Callout type="info">
**10-Minute Warm-Up:**
1. 2 min: Five-finger patterns in C, G, F
2. 3 min: Hanon No. 1 (slow, even)
3. 2 min: C major scale (contrary motion)
4. 2 min: C major arpeggio
5. 1 min: Free play in five-finger position
</Callout>
Remember: exercises are tools, not destinations. The goal is not to play exercises well — it's to play everything else well. Use them to build technique, then spend most of your time on actual music.
